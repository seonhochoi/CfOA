The odds

Born at the bottom, few reach the top

A child born into America’s poorest fifth of families has a 7.5% chance of reaching the richest fifth.

That’s lower than in Canada (13.5%) or Denmark (11.7%). In 2025, 34.5 million Americans lived below the official poverty line. Talent is spread evenly across every zip code. Opportunity isn’t.

Poverty responds to policy. When Congress expanded the Child Tax Credit in 2021, child poverty fell to a record-low 5.2%. When the expansion expired, it more than doubled, to 12.4% in 2022. Choices made in Washington decide how many kids grow up poor.

Our platform

Four planks, four asks

Each plank explains why the issue matters and names exactly what we want elected officials to do about it.

Paying for it

Scale back the top 1% tax cuts

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, signed July 4, 2025, made the 2017 tax cuts permanent. Its benefits run steeply uphill. The nonpartisan Tax Policy Center finds the poorest fifth of households get about $150 in 2026, while the richest 0.1% get about $286,000. Without the law, the top income tax rate would have returned to 39.6% this year.

Our ask

Congress should bring the top income tax rate back to 39.6% and put that money into a fund for the three programs below.

Ages 0 to 5

Start early

The years before kindergarten shape everything after. Nobel-winning economist James Heckman found that high-quality birth-to-five programs for disadvantaged children return about 13% per child, per year through better schooling, health, and earnings. Parents gain too, because they can work while their children learn.

Our ask

Fully fund Head Start and Early Head Start so every eligible child has a seat, and give states matching grants to offer free pre-K to 3- and 4-year-olds from low-income families.

Working parents

Make child care affordable

Child care cost an average of $13,184 in 2025, a third of a single parent’s median income. In 38 states, a year of infant care costs more than in-state public college tuition. A parent who can’t afford care can’t take the job or the training that would lift the family out of poverty.

Our ask

Expand federal child care assistance so low- and middle-income families never pay more than 7% of their income for child care.

Adults

Train for real careers

Job training works when it’s built around jobs employers need to fill. Randomized trials of sector-based training programs found lasting, double-digit earnings gains. Texas has the proof: San Antonio’s Project QUEST raised participants’ earnings by 15 to 20%, about $6,000 a year, nine to fourteen years after they enrolled.

Our ask

Increase federal funding for job training programs run with community colleges and local employers, and help trainees with costs like transportation and child care so they can finish.
