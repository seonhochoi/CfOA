Equal opportunities for equal citizens.

Americans are under attack like never before. Wealth continues to concentrate among the rich, gouging the public for their own self-interest, building mansions, buying yachts, and making golf courses. The rich pay off politicians to maintain their wealth, creating an ironclad cycle where officials are willfully ignorant of this abuse of the system.

We fight to break the cycle of poverty with increased opportunities for the American people. We’ll pay for it by scaling back the tax cuts for the wealthiest 1% of Americans.

The odds

Born at the bottom, few reach the top

A child born into America’s poorest fifth of families has a 7.5% chance of reaching the richest fifth.

That’s lower than in Canada (13.5%) or Denmark (11.7%). In 2025, 34.5 million Americans lived below the official poverty line. Talent is spread evenly across every zip code. Opportunity isn’t.

Poverty responds to policy. When Congress expanded the Child Tax Credit in 2021, child poverty fell to a record-low 5.2%. When the expansion expired, it more than doubled, to 12.4% in 2022. Choices made in Washington decide how many kids grow up poor.

Sources: Raj Chetty, Lampman Memorial Lecture (chances of reaching the top fifth); Congressional Research Service, “Poverty in 2025” (34.5 million); Columbia Center on Poverty and Social Policy, child poverty in 2021 and 2022.

Our platform

Three planks, three asks

Each plank explains why the issue matters and names what we want elected officials to do about it.

How we pay for it

Cut tax breaks for the rich

Recent tax cuts gave the biggest rewards to the people who needed them least. The wealthiest Americans saved enormous sums, while the poorest families got almost nothing. Every dollar handed out at the top is a dollar that can’t go to schools, child care, or job training.

Our ask: Congress should scale back the tax cuts for the wealthiest 1% of Americans and put that money directly toward fighting poverty.

What we fund

Engage a new War on Poverty

America has fought poverty before, and when the country made it a priority, poverty fell. Today millions of Americans still go without the support they need to get ahead. We want a new national effort built on three things that work: early childhood education, help paying for child care, and job training for adults.

Our ask: Fund high-quality preschool for children from low-income families, help working parents afford child care, and pay for job training programs that partner with local employers.

What it achieves

Break the cycle

Too many children are born into poverty and never get out. They have the talent. What they lack are the chances other kids get. When a child starts school ready to learn, a parent can keep a job, and an adult can train for a better one, the whole family moves up, and the next generation starts ahead instead of behind.

Our ask: Treat these programs as a long-term commitment. Protect their funding from year to year, and hold them accountable for results so every dollar helps families climb.
